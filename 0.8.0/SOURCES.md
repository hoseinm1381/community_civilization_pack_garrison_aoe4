Research date: **2026-10-18, Asia/Tehran**. Research notes and proposed dialogue, not an audio release. IDs below are the stable references used in the scripts. Access means the stated material was inspected; it does not imply that a whole book or every dictionary entry was read. No existing performance is licensed or supplied by this dossier.

## Language and pronunciation

**L01 — Gong, Xun (2019), “Chinese loans in Old Vietnamese with a sesquisyllabic phonology,” Journal of Language Relationship 17(1/2), 55–72.** [Publisher PDF](https://www.jolr.ru/files/%28262%29jlr2019-17-1-2%2855-72%29.pdf), [SOAS bibliographic record](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/output/391549/chinese-loans-in-old-vietnamese-with-a-sesquisyllabic-phonology). Inspected publisher full text, especially pp. 55 and 58–61, §§1.3–1.4. Supports preinitials, distinction between clusters and minor syllables, and uncertainty about the language date of Đại báo. The PDF's printed first-page running header disagrees with the repository's 2019 publication metadata; cite the repository metadata and DOI **10.31826/jlr-2019-171-209**, not the stray header. SOAS direct open returned 403; its indexed abstract was available. This is not a recovered spoken-command corpus.

**L02 — Fernandes, Gonçalo & Carlos Assunção (2017), “First codification of Vietnamese by 17th-century missionaries: the description of tones and the influence of Portuguese on Vietnamese orthography,” Histoire Épistémologie Langage 39(1), 155–176.** [Persée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/hel_0750-8069_2017_num_39_1_3592), [accessible paper PDF](https://cvdvn.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/fernandes_assuno_hel2017_39_01_155-176.pdf). Full text available and inspected for alphabet, tones and regional interpretation, particularly pp. 167–169. Supports a six-tone description and disagreement over the regional composition of Rhodes's material. Does not justify treating it as unmixed seventeenth-century Hanoi speech. DOI **10.1051/hel/2017390108**; Persée also assigns **10.3406/hel.2017.3592**.

**L03 — Trần Trọng Dương, Nguyễn Trãi Quốc Âm Từ Điển, explanatory notes and introduction.** [Explanatory notes](https://nomfoundation.org/nom-tools/Nguyen-Trai-Quoc-Am-Tu-Dien/Explanatory-Notes?uiLang=vn), [introduction](https://nomfoundation.org/nom-tools/Nguyen-Trai-Quoc-Am-Tu-Dien/Introduction?uiLang=vn). Inspected the notes on modern transcription versus reconstruction, dictionary method and references to the later printed witness. Use this for lexical research methods and the distinction between readable transcription and historical sound, not blanket attestation of the script bank. Individual script words without an inspected entry remain unverified.

Also inspected the [chăn/chăng entry page](https://www.nomfoundation.org/nom-tools/Nguyen-Trai-Quoc-Am-Tu-Dien?item=ch%C4%83n&uiLang=en): it distinguishes negative and sentence-final interrogative uses of chăng, and the herd-tending verb chăn. Literary evidence does not date these uses to A1 or establish the complete drafted questions.

**L04 — Kirby, James P. (2011), “Vietnamese (Hanoi Vietnamese),” Journal of the International Phonetic Association 41(3), 381–392.** [Publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/vietnamese-hanoi-vietnamese/31774A6EB2D510A1AAC7B0027FE520F4), [paper PDF inspected](https://thinkingasart.philograph.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/kirby2011vietnamese.pdf). DOI **10.1017/S0025100311000181**. Inspected consonants, finals and tones, pp. 382–387. This describes a modern Hanoi speaker; it supplies a modern rehearsal baseline only. Some extracted IPA characters are corrupted, so do not copy the extraction as phonetic truth.

**L05 — Rhodes, Alexandre de (1651), Dictionarium annamiticum lusitanum et latinum, Rome.** [BnF catalogue](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark%3A/12148/cb312064817.public), [University of Vienna digitization index](https://china-bibliographie.univie.ac.at/2010/06/03/rhodes-dictionarium/). Bibliographic/catalogue information inspected; this run did **not** collate the entire original scan or verify every headword. Original entries cited through L01 are explicitly indirect evidence. Essential later primary-source check, not a claim that the present lines occur in 1651.

**L06 — Ferlus, Michel (2004), “The origin of tones in Viet-Muong,” conference proceedings, pp. 297–313.** [SEAlang indexed text](https://sealang.net/sala/archives/pdf8/ferlus2004origin.pdf). Indexed introduction inspected; direct PDF open failed. Useful for distinguishing historical tone categories from present pitch contours and for identifying competing accounts of tonogenesis. Not used to assign absolute eighth-century pitch values or exact dates to tone changes. Earlier `pdf4` mirror failed too.

## Historical setting and occupations

**H01 — UNESCO, Central Sector of the Imperial Citadel of Thang Long–Hanoi.** [Property description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1328). Institutional description inspected. Supports the Red River setting, seventh-century antecedents and eleventh-century Lý citadel. Archaeology identifies a cultural setting, not dialogue or a uniform accent.

**H02 — UNESCO, Yen Tu–Vinh Nghiem–Con Son, Kiep Bac complex.** [Property description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1732). Description and statement of significance inspected. Supports thirteenth-century Trần/Trúc Lâm religious context. Does not establish a standard prayer for all monks, all ages or an actual sound for a game conversion event.

**H03 — Vietnam Department of Cultural Heritage, Di tích lịch sử Bạch Đằng.** [Site description](https://dsvh.gov.vn/di-tich-lich-su-bach-dang-2960). Inspected archaeological/historical site account. Supports a riverine military setting and stake-field associations. Local traditions are not treated as verbatim thirteenth-century commands.

**H04 — Vietnam Military History Museum, Bạch Đằng Giang – Nơi khí thiêng sông núi hội tụ (23 June 2026).** [Museum account](https://btlsqsvn.mod.gov.vn/tin-tuc/chi-tiet/bach-dang-giang-noi-khi-thieng-song-nui-hoi-tu-a7e6cabf-6f02-42ee-85ea-6163d9ac0599). Indexed institutional account inspected: associates naval victories with 938, 981 and 1288. Those dates anchor background only; no script quotes its patriotic prose.

**H05 — Vietnam National Museum of History, Vũ khí cha ông ta: Hỏa khí.** [Museum-hosted article](https://baotanglichsu.vn/VI/Articles/3096/6174/vu-khi-cha-ong-ta-hoa-khi.html). Full page inspected. A popular historical overview, not an artifact catalogue or primary weapons manual. It contains a chronological problem connecting Hồ Nguyên Trừng with a late-sixteenth-century conclusion; do not use that sentence to date his life. Used only for broad late-medieval/early-modern gunpowder context. Exact matchlock loading terminology remains a review item.

**H06 — Hưng Yên Provincial Library, Phố Hiến, thương cảng lớn nhất của Đàng Ngoài.** [Library article](https://thuvienhungyen.vn/dat-va-nguoi-hung-yen/pho-hien--thuong-cang-lon-nhat-cua-dang-ngoai.html). Indexed institutional account inspected, including seventeenth-century foreign shipping and a quoted 1688 description. Supports late-period commerce, not a generic medieval trader's accent. Do not import modern quoted slogans into early banks.

**H07 — Imperial Citadel of Thang Long, Introduction.** [Site-management account](https://hoangthanhthanglong.vn/en/introduction/). Indexed introduction inspected. Supports Lý/Trần/Lê capital continuity. Does not date a linguistic change.

**H08 — K. W. Taylor (2013), A History of the Vietnamese.** [Cambridge catalogue and contents](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/a-history-of-the-vietnamese/2269255F3CF32ECD6827399643AB68D3). ## How sources are applied to scripts

Every complete spoken sentence is **original creative adaptation** unless explicitly marked otherwise. Source IDs in a line establish historical setting, vocabulary-research background or production rationale, **not sentence attestation**. `historical_ipa: null` is intentional when a complete sentence has no defensible reconstruction. The separate pronunciation lexicon contains only bounded examples and preserves source notation.

No YouTube performance, film, commercial voice actor, AoE2 bank, modern newsreader or stock AoE4 dialogue has been copied into the new scripts. Historical texts and modern editions have separate rights; the dossier contains brief scholarly examples and original writing rather than reproduced chapters. Future human/TTS recording requires its own performance/voice rights and pronunciation review.
