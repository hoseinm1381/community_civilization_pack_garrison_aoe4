# Vietnamese voices, approximately AD 700–1700

## Research conclusion

Use a **Northern Đại Việt cultural center** and four chronological voice banks, with a stable actor for each role. Create a perceptible progression through vocabulary choices, social register and only those historical sound features that can be defended. Do not substitute modern Mandarin for early Vietnamese, generic “ancient Asian” delivery, or modern Hanoi with randomly added consonants.

The available evidence is uneven. No recording survives from these centuries. A few reconstructed words cannot establish a complete early language. This dossier therefore supplies complete **creative recording drafts**, a bounded pronunciation research lexicon and explicit review gates. It does not claim to have reconstructed a thousand years of spoken Vietnamese. Source IDs resolve in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## Four banks and their limits

These are **art-direction ranges**, not universal scholarly period boundaries and not game technology dates. The same person does not biologically age by several centuries. Runtime routing must follow verified unit/upgrade ownership and the player's intended chronology later.

## What historical phonology does and does not support

Gong distinguishes consonant-liquid clusters from minor-syllable preinitials in Đại báo. Its surviving print is around 1730, while proposed dates for its underlying language range from the twelfth through fifteenth centuries. His notation combines reconstructed initials with projected modern rimes; it must not be relabeled as complete phonetic IPA. The indirect Rhodes example **blời** corresponds to modern **trời**; an Old Vietnamese **\*pl-** initial is a reconstruction, not a license to replace every modern `tr`. [L01](https://www.jolr.ru/files/%28262%29jlr2019-17-1-2%2855-72%29.pdf), pp. 55, 58–61.

The Nguyễn Trãi dictionary deliberately distinguishes readable modern transcription from proposed older sound forms, including **pləj²** for trời. Its edition uses later textual witnesses, so literary attribution and surviving-copy date must remain separate. [L03](https://nomfoundation.org/nom-tools/Nguyen-Trai-Quoc-Am-Tu-Dien/Explanatory-Notes?uiLang=vn)

Rhodes's 1651 dictionary and grammar provide a late anchor. Fernandes and Assunção describe the six-tone account and competing Northern/Central interpretations of its dialect basis. It cannot be adopted wholesale as a pure Northern standard. [L02](https://www.persee.fr/doc/hel_0750-8069_2017_num_39_1_3592?pageId=T9_157), pp. 167–169. The BnF record confirms the 1651 publication and grammar component; this run has not independently collated every headword. [L05](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark%3A/12148/cb312064817.public)

Ferlus's introduction contrasts historical explanations of tonal development. The production implication is to keep abstract tone ancestry separate from exact pitch and phonation at a particular date. Do not obtain A1 by simply removing modern tone marks. [L06](https://sealang.net/sala/archives/pdf8/ferlus2004origin.pdf) Direct PDF retrieval failed; this point rests on its indexed introduction only.

For rehearsals, Kirby describes **modern Hanoi**, including tone/voice-quality interaction and unreleased stop finals. His distinction between modern colloquial and literary pronunciation is useful; neither is automatically medieval. Preserve lexical tones in whispered and shouted variants. `s`, `x`, `tr`, `ch`, `d`, `gi`, `r` should not undergo a global “ancient accent” substitution. [L04](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/vietnamese-hanoi-vietnamese/31774A6EB2D510A1AAC7B0027FE520F4), pp. 381–387.

### Pronunciation policy

- Modern quốc ngữ in the bank is a readable script and semantic reference. It is not a claim that this spelling or pronunciation was used in AD 700.
- Every line has a modern rehearsal instruction and historical-pronunciation status. `null` means unresolved, not silently approved modern speech.
- [PRONUNCIATION_LEXICON.json](PRONUNCIATION_LEXICON.json) separates source forms, bounded IPA-like features and modern rehearsal examples. Asterisk means reconstructed, not directly recorded.
- Keep source notation and tone-category superscripts intact. Do not invent numerical pitch contours for the early ages or equate superscript `2` with a universal tone height.
- Historical changes apply word by word after review. Whole-line IPA is deferred where the evidence is insufficient.

## Social roles and address

The player is an interface abstraction; a clicked villager is not necessarily addressing a king. Default civilian speech uses short, pronoun-light acknowledgements, practical verbs and occasional `tôi`. Military speech favors collective action and clear intent. `ta` is reserved for suitable self/group statements, not a universal pronoun replacement.

Do not pepper every early line with `bẩm`, `tiểu nhân`, `nô tỳ`, `bần tăng`, `bệ hạ` or `tuân chỉ` to simulate age. These can encode status, relationship or genre that the game does not establish. Royal self-reference such as `trẫm` is inappropriate for ordinary units. Ancient-looking `mỗ`, `mựa` and `chăng` require entry-level evidence and context; this draft does not spray them across the bank. This is an editorial choice, not a claim that all these terms are modern.

Neither women nor peasants should be performed as childish, servile or comic. The two civilian actors share occupations and sentence meanings, with individual timbre rather than exaggerated gender stereotypes. Regional diversity inside the historic population does not justify switching every actor to a different modern dialect as the player ages up.

## Religion, work and warfare

**Monks.** The Yên Tử heritage description links Trúc Lâm with thirteenth-century Trần rulers and a syncretic religious setting. That supports a calm, locally rooted A3 identity; it does not establish identical ritual speech for A1 or every religious unit. [H02](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1732) Use original healing, travel and relic-care lines. Conversion is a game mechanic: avoid a mock “magical chant,” a fabricated historical mantra or the assumption that healing is an attested battlefield rite. No modern ceremonial recording is reused.

**Farmers and builders.** Favor concrete tools/materials: rice, wood, stone, bamboo, grain, timber joints, repair and carrying. Tool words are modern-script creative candidates until historical entries are checked. Do not change game food jobs into literal rice work when a unit is gathering berries or hunting. Sex does not restrict the task list.

**Officials and merchants.** Distinguish tax collection, accounting and supervision from ordinary trade. Avoid presenting the donor's Chinese official title or donor accent as Vietnamese evidence. The provincial-library account of Phố Hiến supports a busy seventeenth-century trading background. [H06](https://thuvienhungyen.vn/dat-va-nguoi-hung-yen/pho-hien--thuong-cang-lon-nhat-cua-dang-ngoai.html) Early traders should not mention Phố Hiến or a particular foreign company without a dated reason.

**Infantry, bows and cavalry.** Use concise coordination rather than long patriotic speeches. `cung` and `nỏ` remain distinct in scripts; mounted troops avoid European knightly oaths. A spear/fire-lance shared actor can share general orders, while equipment-specific phrases stay gated to the actual weapon. `gươm` is a research candidate with historical lexical background in L01; that does not attest the drafted full sentences.

**Gunpowder and siege.** The museum's popular overview is useful context but includes a chronological problem, documented under H05. It cannot validate every modeled weapon or a particular loading drill. [H05](https://baotanglichsu.vn/VI/Articles/3096/6174/vu-khi-cha-ong-ta-hoa-khi.html) Gunner and siege lines describe only visible/supported actions. Exact weapon subtype, ignition and loading vocabulary require specialist review. Do not put handcannon dialogue into playable A1 merely because all four research banks exist.

**Elephants and ships.** Human riders/commanders speak; elephants, horses and ship hulls do not acquire human pain cries. Whether a visible crew actually has an independent speaker must be traced. Special handcannon/archer elephant crews use their own weapon-context filters. Naval fish/trade/military profiles separate occupations; transport orders are conditional on a real transport consumer. All proposed nonverbal human efforts remain separate from animal calls, hull impacts and shared combat effects.

## AoE4-style performance, as a proposal

The developer interview supports native casting and cultural consultation. [G01](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2021/04/10/age-of-empires-4-interview/) SIDE's project account describes historical-dialect production. [G02](https://qacmsptwside.ptw.com/side/?page_id=28331) Neither source is a public per-context recording manual. The following are our production choices:

Short responses, recognizable role timbres, restrained idle delivery, energetic combat without flattening tones, quiet concealed delivery only for a supported context, and one clear responder for groups. The three takes change intention, pace and breath; they are fresh performances of identical words, not pitch-shifted duplicates. Ages change language/register targets while preserving actor identity. More complex wording is not automatically later or more historically accurate.

They must approve each sentence's idiomatic meaning, pronouns, lexical dates, tones, occupational usage and any proposed historical realization. Complete original drafts are supplied now, rather than pretending that source links make them ancient quotations. Resolve comments in stable line IDs and retain this dossier as the preparation baseline.
